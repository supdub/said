## Design Context

### Users

VoiceKey is for bilingual writers, developers, and keyboard-first power users who dictate into whatever Windows app already has focus. They care about speed, privacy, reliable text insertion, and a shortcut that stays out of their way.

### Product job

Turn a quick spoken thought into text at the current caret with the smallest possible interruption. The experience begins with a trustworthy installer and a short microphone/shortcut rehearsal, then recedes into a tray icon and a compact, non-focus-stealing status instrument.

### Brand personality

Quiet, tactile, fast.

VoiceKey should feel like a precise physical key: immediate when pressed, calm while working, and definite when it finishes. Copy is brief and literal. Errors explain what happened and the next action without jokes or blame.

### Aesthetic direction

- Premium native Windows utility, not a web dashboard and not a clone of Typeless.
- Adaptive light and dark palettes with ink-tinted neutrals and one rare signal accent: vermilion.
- The signature mark is a **voice cursor**: a text caret whose two offset pulses suggest speech becoming typed text.
- The overlay is a compact horizontal instrument with a strong state signal, live waveform, restrained border, and soft elevation in light mode. Dark mode uses surface contrast instead of a heavy shadow.
- Typography uses the native Segoe family for instant rendering and excellent Chinese/English coverage. Hierarchy comes from weight, spacing, and contrast rather than many type sizes.
- Layout follows a 4-point spacing grid. Interactive targets are at least 44 pixels high. Focus is always visible.
- Motion is functional: immediate press feedback, a 60–80 ms live meter cadence, and brief ease-out state changes. Reduced-motion users get state changes without spatial animation.

### Core experience

1. A per-user installer presents the real product name and icon, installs without administrator access, offers Start Menu and optional desktop/startup shortcuts, and launches setup.
2. Setup explains local processing, confirms microphone input with a live meter, rehearses the shortcut, and gives the user a small field for a first dictation.
3. Day to day, tapping the shortcut starts listening immediately. The overlay never takes focus. Tapping again switches instantly to transcribing, then confirms insertion or provides a useful recovery state.
4. Double-clicking the tray icon opens VoiceKey. The tray menu exposes status, settings/setup, the model folder, and quit.

### Design principles

1. **Caret first.** Protect the user’s current context and never steal focus during dictation.
2. **Signal, not spectacle.** Show microphone energy and task state clearly; keep decoration quiet.
3. **Fast twice.** Optimize measured inference time and perceived responsiveness at the moment of the shortcut.
4. **Local is visible.** Make offline/private processing a calm trust cue, not marketing noise.
5. **Every state earns its words.** Use specific labels such as “Listening”, “Turning speech into text”, and “Transcript copied”.

### Avoid

- Generic blue/purple AI gradients, glowing orbs, glassmorphism, excessive rounded cards, oversized headings, and novelty animation.
- A permanently large window or an overlay that competes with the user’s work.
- Ambiguous controls such as “OK”, unlabeled icon-only actions, or color-only state communication.
- Pure black/gray surfaces, low-contrast secondary copy, or mechanically inverted dark mode.
