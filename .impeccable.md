## Design Context

### Users

SAID is for bilingual writers, developers, and keyboard-first Windows users who dictate into the app that already has focus. They care about immediate invocation, privacy, control over whether text is verbatim or polished, and a utility that disappears when idle.

### Product job

Turn a spoken thought into usable text at the current caret with the smallest possible interruption. One shortcut starts listening; the same shortcut finishes. Stable phrases begin recognition repair and spoken-language cleanup while the user continues speaking. Optional app-aware composition remains on the PC, and focus is never stolen during dictation.

### Brand personality

Terse, tactile, controlled.

SAID should feel like a physical black key or a definite ink mark: immediate when pressed, calm while listening, and conclusive when finished. Copy is brief and literal. It does not joke or hype model intelligence, and it names Exact, Clean, or the current Adapt profile whenever rewriting is active.

### Aesthetic direction

- A premium native Windows utility built from Ink and Bone, with no chromatic brand accent.
- The signature mark is three speech dots tightening into a text caret.
- Setup is asymmetric and editorial, with generous empty space, firm 8–12 px radii, and clear monochrome hierarchy.
- The everyday overlay is a compact Raised Ink instrument in both app themes. Bone waveform and glyph changes—not color—distinguish state.
- Native UI uses Segoe UI Variable where available and Segoe UI as fallback for reliable Chinese/English rendering.
- Motion explains transformation: live microphone energy while listening, dots resolving into a caret while transcribing, and a held final glyph. Reduced-motion users get state changes without spatial travel.

### Core experience

1. A per-user installer presents SAID and its mark, installs without administrator access, offers Start Menu and optional desktop/startup shortcuts, and launches setup.
2. Setup explains local processing, confirms microphone input with a live meter, rehearses the shortcut, and prepares the first dictation.
3. Tapping the shortcut starts listening immediately. The overlay never takes focus. Stable phrases are refined behind recognition; if live typing is enabled, revisions replace only the exact SAID-owned suffix. Tapping again settles the thought. During final Adapt, the same shortcut immediately keeps the complete Clean draft.
4. Double-clicking the tray icon opens SAID. The tray menu exposes status, Exact/Clean/Adapt, application behavior, Setup & settings, the model folder, and Quit SAID.

### Design principles

1. **Caret first.** Protect the user’s current context and never steal focus during dictation.
2. **Form carries state.** Every state gets a distinct glyph and explicit label; color is never required.
3. **Control before polish.** Exact, Clean, and Adapt are explicit. Rewriting is local, bounded to SAID-owned text, reversible, and safely falls back to the complete Clean draft.
4. **Black as material.** Ink is the object, Bone is the impression, and their mixtures are the entire brand palette.
5. **Motion has a job.** Animation shows microphone energy or speech becoming text, then stops.

### Avoid

- Chromatic accents, gradients, glows, glass effects, microphone glyphs, speech bubbles, and decorative waveforms.
- Excessive rounded cards, oversized headings, a permanent dashboard, or an overlay that competes with the user’s work.
- Ambiguous controls, icon-only recovery actions, color-only state, low-contrast muted copy, and mechanically inverted dark mode.
